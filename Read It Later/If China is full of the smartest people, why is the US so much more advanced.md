What do you mean by “advanced?”

What are the criteria?

How do you measure a country's “advancement?”

So usually there are a few measurements when people talk about a country’s “advancement.”

First, the economy. This is usually measured by the size and GDP per capita.

China is currently the second-largest economy in the world and will probably surpass the US in the next couple of decades. Not to mention, China had experienced colonialism, civil unrest, 8 years of the Sino-Japanese War, and various political movements during the Mao era. In comparison, the US hasn’t seen any war on its soil after the Civil War. Even during WWII, when Asia and Europe were burned to the ground, the US managed to grow its economy. So you guys got lucky. You profited from the war while the rest of us barely survived.

China is a bit behind in GDP per capita, but it also has a lot of people. If the US had 1.5 billion people, its GDP would be at a third-world-country level.

And I would say China is actually ahead of the US in its infrastructure and, in many cases, quality of life.

This is China’s high-speed railway map.

![[Read It Later/attachments/7af40a0bfc4cd8143254e20e93754bcd_MD5.jpg]]

As for the US… noted only the red and yellow lines are considered “high-speed” (120–240Mph).

![[Read It Later/attachments/a558cf518ba63a227cab9fe114e86c4b_MD5.png]]

And there’s the quality of life. Western journalists LOVE to compare China’s rural country villages to big cities of the US. Of course, if you compare the poorest in China with regular folks in the US, it would appear that the US is a lot better. But if we’re comparing apples to apples, big cities to big cities, there really isn’t that much of a difference. If anything, I think Chinese cities are better than US cities because of how walkable they are, how convenient public transportation is, and how we barely have any food deserts. The poorest of people in the cities have access to fresh produce. There’s a reason obesity isn’t nearly as bad in China compared to the US.

The US has just recently started to implement phone scanning for payment in various stores. When I was in China back in 2018, I could go out and have fun, shopping, and having dinner without bringing my wallet because I could pay with my phone everywhere, even at the farmer’s market.

China has its own version of universal health care. It’s not perfect. Obviously, no system is perfect, especially for a country as large as China. But it is there. It exists. If nothing else, at least the Chinese government believes a person shouldn’t die because they’re too poor to pay for health care. I say that’s more advanced than the US.

The last time I went back to China before the pandemic, I did not feel any significant difference between China and the US in quality of life and technological advances in everyday life.

But but but, what about human rights?

I mean… do you really want to talk about human rights right now? after the overturn of Roe V. Wade? How many countries in the world still limit a woman’s reproductive rights?

And last not least, there is global influence. And that, the US takes the crown. Nobody can beat the US on its military power and Hollywood's cultural output. But the thing is, China doesn’t really want that kind of global influence. China doesn’t want to be the world police and spend money on a bloated military. It’d much rather spend its money on infrastructure and technology. China has its One Belt One Road initiative, but it’s more about expanding trade and less about “dominance.” China doesn’t want to be a “global force for good.” China just wants to take care of its own citizens and make a lot of money.

Honestly, I don’t think the US is more advanced than China, let alone “so much more” advanced. I think China is well on its way to surpass the US in every way other than military.

So perhaps let’s not write answers about China traditionally doesn’t care about math because you read one wiki page about Confucianism.
